Cookies are small bits of data stored in your browser that help websites remember things like your login, preferences, and settings to create a smoother browsing experience.

But not all cookies are harmless — some are used to track your activity across websites, collect personal data, and build detailed profiles for advertising and analytics.

With Cookie Jar, you can manage your cookie preferences at a glance, and set your default preferences so you don't have to choose between convenience and privacy.

Session Cookies

Session cookies are temporary cookies that help websites remember you while you use them — like keeping you logged in or saving your cart. They disappear once you close your browser.

e.g. Amazon keeps your shopping cart intact while you browse; YouTube remembers your video position within a single visit.

Authentication Cookies

Authentication cookies help websites know that you're logged in. They store a secure ID so you can stay signed in while browsing without entering your password on every page.

e.g. Google keeps you signed in across Gmail, Drive, and Docs; Spotify remembers your account as you navigate between pages.

Tracking Cookies

Tracking cookies follow your activity across websites to collect data about your behavior, interests, and browsing habits — often to personalize ads and analytics.

e.g. The Facebook Pixel records pages you visit so Facebook can retarget you with ads; Google's ad network tracks searches and site visits across millions of websites.

Third-Party Cookies

Third-party cookies are set by websites other than the one you're currently visiting. They're commonly used for cross-site tracking, targeted advertising, and embedded content like social media buttons.

e.g. A Facebook "Like" button on a news site sets a cookie from facebook.com; a Google Maps embed on a restaurant's page sets cookies from google.com.

Analytics Cookies

Analytics cookies measure how you interact with a website — which pages you visit, how long you stay, and what you click. While site owners use this data to improve their product, many analytics tools also aggregate your behavior into profiles that can be shared or sold.

e.g. Google Analytics tracks page views and session duration on most websites; Hotjar records click heatmaps and session replays to study user behavior.

Advertising Cookies

Advertising cookies follow your browsing habits across sites to build a profile of your interests and serve you targeted ads. They are typically placed by ad networks, not the site you're visiting, and can track you even after you leave.

e.g. Google AdSense tailors banner ads based on your search and browsing history; Meta's Pixel shows you Facebook ads for products you viewed on other sites.

Functional Cookies

Functional cookies remember your preferences — like your chosen language, region, or layout — to give you a more personalized experience. Unlike tracking cookies, they typically stay within the site you're visiting and don't follow you elsewhere.

e.g. Netflix saves your preferred language and subtitle settings; X (Twitter) remembers whether you prefer dark or light mode.

Unknown Cookies

Unknown cookies don't match any recognized category — they may come from third-party scripts, plugins, or services whose purpose hasn't been identified. Since their intent is unclear, it's generally safer to block them until you know what they're doing.

e.g. A live chat widget, A/B testing script, or affiliate tracking pixel whose origin or data usage isn't clearly disclosed by the site.

Privacy Policy

Effective: May 26, 2026

1. Overview

Cookie Jar is built with privacy as a core principle. The Extension operates entirely within your browser and does not transmit any personal data to external servers. This policy explains what data the Extension accesses, how it is used, and how it is stored.

2. Data We Access

The Extension accesses cookie metadata (name, domain, expiry, and flags such as HttpOnly and Secure) from your browser in order to classify and selectively block cookies. Cookie values are never read, stored, or transmitted. The Extension does not access your browsing history, form data, passwords, or any other personal information.

3. Local Storage Only

All data generated by the Extension — including your cookie category preferences, custom rules, per-domain overrides, and the blocked-cookie log used for Statistics — is stored exclusively in your browser using chrome.storage.local. This data never leaves your device and is not accessible to Snickerdoodles or any third party.

4. Cookie Classification

Cookies are classified locally using pattern-matching rules and heuristics bundled within the Extension. No cookie names, domains, or metadata are sent to any external server for classification or analysis. Classification happens entirely on your device.

5. No Third-Party Analytics or Advertising

The Extension does not integrate any third-party analytics services, advertising networks, or crash-reporting tools. We do not use your data for advertising purposes and we do not sell, rent, or share your data with any third party.

6. Permissions

The Extension requests only the browser permissions necessary to function: access to cookies (to read and block them), access to the active tab's URL (to apply per-domain rules), and storage (to save your preferences locally). No broader permissions are requested or used.

7. Children's Privacy

The Extension is not directed at children under the age of 13. We do not knowingly collect any information from children.
