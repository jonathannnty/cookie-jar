Cookie Jar

Tailor your cookies!

Cookies are small bits of data stored in your browser that help websites remember things like your login, preferences, and settings to provide a smoother browsing experience.

But not all cookies are created equally — some are used to track your activity across websites and sell that information to advertisers. With Cookie Jar, you can choose between necessary and optional cookies to protect your privacy — without breaking your browsing.

Session Cookies

Session cookies are temporary cookies that help websites remember you while you use them — like keeping you logged in or saving your cart. They disappear once you close your browser.

Authentication Cookies

Authentication cookies keep you signed in to websites even as you navigate between pages. Without them you'd need to re-enter your password on every click. If stolen, attackers can log into your accounts without your password.

Tracking Cookies

Tracking cookies follow your activity across websites to build detailed profiles about you. In 2025, 93.7 billion stolen tracking cookies were found on the dark web — linked to Google, YouTube, and other major platforms.

Third-Party Cookies

Third-party cookies are set by a domain different from the site you're on — embedded social buttons, videos, or ad networks. They can track your browsing across many sites simultaneously.

Analytics Cookies

Analytics cookies measure how visitors use a website — which pages are popular, where people drop off, and how long they spend. Less intrusive than tracking, though aggregate data can still identify individuals.

Advertising Cookies

Advertising cookies deliver targeted ads based on your browsing history and measure ad conversions. They are often shared across hundreds of advertising networks.

Functional Cookies

Functional cookies save your preferences — like language, theme, or font size — so you don't reset them every visit. Generally low-risk, though they can persist as a long-lived fingerprint.

Unknown Cookies

These cookies don't match any known pattern — could be custom app cookies, proprietary tracking, or site-specific data. When uncertain, consider blocking them from sites you don't fully trust.

Safe vs. Unsafe Cookies

Safe Cookies
  • Session cookies
  • Authentication
  • Security tokens
  • Consent records
  • Load balancing
Unsafe Cookies
  • Cross-site tracking
  • Advertising profiles
  • Third-party trackers
  • Behavioral analytics
  • Zombie cookies
